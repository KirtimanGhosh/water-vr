The equations behind every motion

Every molecular position and orientation comes from integrating Newton's equations with the forces below. Phase boundaries come from the IAPWS correlations. The beaker surface follows linear capillary–gravity wave theory.

1. The molecule: TIP4P/Ice

r(O–H) = 0.9572 Å, ∠HOH = 104.52°, q_H = +0.5897 e, q_M = −1.1794 e at d_OM = 0.1577 Å on the bisector
Lennard-Jones on O: σ = 3.1668 Å, ε/k_B = 106.1 K  (melting point of ice Ih in the model: 272.2 K)
Massless site: r_M = r_O + a(r_H1 − r_O) + a(r_H2 − r_O), a = d_OM / (2 r_OH cos(θ/2)); F_O += (1−2a)F_M, F_H += aF_M
Abascal, Sanz, García Fernández & Vega, J. Chem. Phys. 122, 234511 (2005).

2. Forces

U = Σ_{i<j} [ Σ_{a,b∈{H1,H2,M}} k_e q_a q_b V_DSF(r_ab) + 4ε((σ/r_OO)¹² − (σ/r_OO)⁶) ]
V_DSF(r) = erfc(αr)/r − erfc(αR_c)/R_c + [erfc(αR_c)/R_c² + (2α/√π)e^(−α²R_c²)/R_c](r − R_c),  α = 0.2 Å⁻¹, R_c = 8.5 Å
Long-range LJ: U_tail/N = (8/3)πρεσ³[(1/3)(σ/R_c)⁹ − (σ/R_c)³],   P_tail = (16/3)πρ²εσ³[(2/3)(σ/R_c)⁹ − (σ/R_c)³]
Fennell & Gezelter, J. Chem. Phys. 124, 234104 (2006).

3. Motion, temperature and pressure

m_k d²r_k/dt² = −∇_k U + Σ_c λ_c ∇_k σ_c,   σ_c = |r_a − r_b|² − d_ab² = 0  (velocity Verlet + SHAKE/RATTLE, Δt = 2 fs)
Thermostat (Bussi–Donadio–Parrinello 2007): v → αv, α² = c + (K̄/N_fK)(1−c)(R₁² + Σ R_i²) + 2R₁√(c(1−c)K̄/(N_fK)), c = e^(−Δt/τ)
Barostat (stochastic cell rescaling, Bernetti & Bussi 2020): dε = −(β_T/τ_p)(P₀ − P − k_BT/V)dt + √(2k_BTβ_T dt/(Vτ_p)) ξ,  ε = ln V
Pressure (molecular virial): P = [⅔K_com + ⅓ Σ_{i<j} F_ij·R_ij]/V + P_tail

4. Phase boundaries of real water (IAPWS R14-08 and SR1-86)

Ice Ih melting: p/p_t = 1 + Σ_{i=1}^{3} a_i(1 − θ^{b_i}), θ = T/273.16 K, p_t = 611.657 Pa; a = (1.19539337×10⁶, 8.08183159×10⁴, 3.33826860×10³), b = (3, 25.75, 103.75)
Ice III: p/208.566 MPa = 1 − 0.2999481(1 − θ⁶⁰), θ = T/251.165 K
Ice V: p/350.1 MPa = 1 − 1.18721(1 − θ⁸), θ = T/256.164 K  ·  Ice VI: p/632.4 MPa = 1 − 1.07476(1 − θ^4.6), θ = T/273.31 K
Ice VII: ln(p/2216 MPa) = 1.73683(1 − θ⁻¹) − 0.0544606(1 − θ⁵) + 0.806106×10⁻⁷(1 − θ²²), θ = T/355 K
Sublimation: ln(p/p_t) = θ⁻¹ Σ a_i θ^{b_i}; a = (−21.2144006, 27.3203819, −6.10598130), b = (0.00333333333, 1.20666667, 1.70333333)
Vapour pressure: ln(p/p_c) = (T_c/T)(a₁τ + a₂τ^1.5 + a₃τ³ + a₄τ^3.5 + a₅τ⁴ + a₆τ^7.5), τ = 1 − T/T_c; T_c = 647.096 K, p_c = 22.064 MPa
Checked in this app against the IAPWS check values: Ih 260 K → 138.268 MPa; III 254 K → 268.685 MPa; V 265 K → 479.640 MPa; VI 320 K → 1356.76 MPa; VII 550 K → 6308.71 MPa; sublimation 230 K → 8.94735 Pa.

6. Recorded simulations

Melting: an ice Ih / water interface (432 molecules), overheated to 330 K so the front moves within tens of picoseconds; light-blue molecules moved less than 1.4 Å in 5 ps (still crystalline). Evaporation: a 216-molecule water film in vacuum at 550 K. Both are shown as time-lapse; freezing from supercooled water takes nanoseconds or longer and is not shown.
